\begin{abstract}
This proof document closes the mathematical obligations carried into Phase~2B-R1 for exact decoding on a binary channel with exactly one deletion
and independent substitutions. It proves a strict best-path/aggregate-likelihood
reversal, the exact aggregate likelihood and recurrence, the independent-stream
certificate, a recurrence-relaxed shell bound, and an alignment-consistent shell bound.
It removes a redundant state coordinate from the preliminary exact dynamic program,
improving its worst-case complexity from $O(n^4)$ time and $O(n^3)$ memory to $O(n^3)$
time and $O(n^2)$ rolling memory. It also proves that the cheaper $O(n^2)$ recurrence
relaxation is powerless under the ordinary forward shell-completion frontier, but becomes
strict under a valid nonmonotone tie order. The statements have passed internal exact-arithmetic and implementation cross-checks; independent human proof review, novelty, and empirical usefulness remain separate gates. Phase~2B-R1 changes only artifact provenance and does not alter the mathematical statements.
\end{abstract}

\section{Model and conventions}

Let $X=(X_1,\ldots,X_n)\in\{0,1\}^n$. Independent substitutions act as
$\widetilde X_i=X_i\oplus N_i$, where the $N_i$ are i.i.d. Bernoulli$(p_s)$ and
$0\le p_s<1/2$. Exactly one coordinate $J$ is deleted, with
$\Prb[J=j]=q_j$, $q_j\ge0$, and $\sum_jq_j=1$. The observation
$Y\in\{0,1\}^{n-1}$ is known, as are $p_s$ and $\{q_j\}$. The code
$\C\subseteq\{0,1\}^n$ is arbitrary and has a uniform prior. The complete ML tie set is
\[
 \operatorname{ML}(y)=\argmax_{c\in\C}W(y\mid c).
\]
A scalar output, when needed, is the lexicographically smallest word in this set.

For $x\in\{0,1\}^n$ and $y\in\{0,1\}^{n-1}$, define
\begin{equation}
 d_j(x,y)=\sum_{i=1}^{j-1}\ind\{x_i\ne y_i\}
 +\sum_{i=j+1}^{n}\ind\{x_i\ne y_{i-1}\}.
 \label{eq:dj}
\end{equation}
This is the substitution mismatch count when coordinate $j$ of $x$ is deleted.



\section{Boundary conventions and exact-complexity interpretation}

The main algorithmic regime is $0<p_s<1/2$.  The following boundary conventions prevent
silent overstatement.

\begin{proposition}[Boundary cases and zero deletion weights]
The aggregate likelihood and independent-stream certificate remain valid at $p_s=0$ and
$p_s=1/2$ when powers at the boundary are interpreted directly from the channel law.
At $p_s=0$, only zero-mismatch components have positive mass, and all strict-gain formulas
in this document collapse to non-strict equalities when their displayed factor contains
$p_s$.  At $p_s=1/2$, every substitution pattern of length $n-1$ has the same probability,
so shell order contains no likelihood information and no strict shell-consistency gain is
claimed.  If $q_j=0$, alignment $j$ contributes zero to every candidate likelihood.  It may
be retained as a zero-weight stream or omitted; if omitted, its frontier constraint is not
used in a strengthened bound.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
Conditioning on the deletion coordinate proves the likelihood identity for every
$p_s\in[0,1]$.  The certificate uses only that each unprocessed component is bounded by its
stream frontier, and therefore does not require strict monotonicity.  At $p_s=0$ the only
positive substitution term has mismatch count zero.  At $p_s=1/2$, each length-$(n-1)$
error vector has probability $2^{-(n-1)}$.  A coordinate with $q_j=0$ contributes the zero
function, so deleting that term and its list leaves the objective unchanged.
\end{proof}

All $O(\cdot)$ claims below count exact arithmetic additions and comparisons under a
unit-cost arithmetic model.  For rational channel parameters represented by integer
weights and a rational odds ratio, the operands have $O(n)$ bits for fixed parameters.
Consequently, bit complexity includes the cost of arbitrary-precision arithmetic and is
not asserted to equal the arithmetic-operation count.  The Phase~2B implementation uses exact fixed-capacity 512-bit multiword integers
with overflow detection.  That capacity is sufficient for the frozen Phase~2B parameter
range; no general unbounded-integer implementation claim is made.  Kernel time is
reported empirically.

\section{Exact aggregate likelihood}

\begin{proposition}[Likelihood and sliding recurrence]
For every $x$ and $y$,
\begin{equation}
 W(y\mid x)=\sum_{j=1}^{n}q_jp_s^{d_j(x,y)}(1-p_s)^{n-1-d_j(x,y)}.
 \label{eq:likelihood}
\end{equation}
Moreover,
\begin{align}
 d_1(x,y)&=\sum_{i=2}^{n}\ind\{x_i\ne y_{i-1}\},\\
 d_{j+1}(x,y)&=d_j(x,y)-\ind\{x_{j+1}\ne y_j\}+\ind\{x_j\ne y_j\},
 \qquad 1\le j<n.
 \label{eq:recurrence}
\end{align}
Thus all $n$ components of one candidate likelihood can be evaluated in $O(n)$ time.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
Condition on $J=j$. The $n-1$ surviving coordinates must match $y$, and exactly
$d_j(x,y)$ of them require a substitution. Independence gives the $j$th term in
\eqref{eq:likelihood}; summing over $J$ gives the result. A substitution on the deleted
coordinate has marginalized probability one, so deleting before or after substitutions
is equivalent for the observed law.

When the hypothesized deletion moves from $j$ to $j+1$, every comparison is unchanged
except the comparison between $y_j$ and the two adjacent symbols: the former suffix term
$\ind\{x_{j+1}\ne y_j\}$ is removed and the new prefix term
$\ind\{x_j\ne y_j\}$ is added. This proves \eqref{eq:recurrence}. Computing $d_1$ and
performing $n-1$ constant-time updates gives the stated complexity.
\end{proof}

\section{Strict best-path/aggregate-ML reversal}

For a candidate $x$, define its most probable compatible individual history by
\[
 M_y(x)=\max_{1\le j\le n}q_jp_s^{d_j(x,y)}(1-p_s)^{n-1-d_j(x,y)}.
\]

\begin{theorem}[Strict reversal family]
Assume uniform deletion, $q_j=1/n$, and $0<p_s<1/2$. For $n\ge4$, let
\[
 y=0^{n-3}10,\qquad x_A=0^{n-3}101,\qquad x_B=0^n,
 \qquad \C=\{x_A,x_B\}.
\]
Then $M_y(x_A)>M_y(x_B)$. Nevertheless,
\[
 W(y\mid x_B)>W(y\mid x_A)
 \quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
 np_s>1+2p_s^2.
\]
Consequently, for every fixed $p_s>0$, sufficiently large finite $n$ gives a strict
best-history/ML disagreement.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Deleting the final symbol of $x_A$ gives $y$ with no substitution, hence
\[
 M_y(x_A)=\frac1n(1-p_s)^{n-1}.
\]
Every deletion alignment of $x_B$ leaves $0^{n-1}$, which differs from $y$ in one
coordinate, so
\[
 M_y(x_B)=\frac1n p_s(1-p_s)^{n-2}<M_y(x_A).
\]
For $x_A$, the mismatch counts $0,1,2,3$ have multiplicities
$1,1,1,n-3$, respectively. Aggregation therefore gives
\begin{align*}
 W(y\mid x_B)&=p_s(1-p_s)^{n-2},\\
 W(y\mid x_A)&=\frac1n\bigl[(1-p_s)^{n-1}
 +p_s(1-p_s)^{n-2}+p_s^2(1-p_s)^{n-3}
 +(n-3)p_s^3(1-p_s)^{n-4}\bigr].
\end{align*}
Direct factorization yields
\[
 \frac{n\bigl(W(y\mid x_B)-W(y\mid x_A)\bigr)}{(1-p_s)^{n-4}}
 =(1-2p_s)(np_s-1-2p_s^2).
\]
The first factor is positive, proving the equivalence. For fixed $p_s>0$, any integer
$n>(1+2p_s^2)/p_s$ satisfies the condition.
\end{proof}

\section{Independent-stream certificate}

For alignment $j$, error pattern $e\in\{0,1\}^{n-1}$, and deleted bit $b$, let
$x=\operatorname{Ins}_j(y\oplus e,b)$ and
\[
 \omega_j(e)=q_jp_s^{w_H(e)}(1-p_s)^{n-1-w_H(e)}.
\]
Each stream emits its unique pairs $(e,b)$ in nonincreasing $\omega_j(e)$. After time
$t$, let $\eta_j(t)$ be the largest unprocessed component weight in stream $j$, or zero
if the stream is exhausted. Let $\D_t$ be the distinct candidates generated by time $t$.

\begin{theorem}[Exact independent-stream certificate]
Every completely undiscovered candidate satisfies
\[
 W(y\mid x)\le U_{\mathrm{ind}}(t):=\sum_{j=1}^{n}\eta_j(t).
\]
Assume at least one codeword has been discovered and every discovered codeword has been
scored using its complete likelihood. Define
\[
 s_t^\star=\max_{c\in\C\cap\D_t}W(y\mid c),\qquad
 \mathcal T_t^\star=\{c\in\C\cap\D_t:W(y\mid c)=s_t^\star\}.
\]
If $s_t^\star>U_{\mathrm{ind}}(t)$, then $\mathcal T_t^\star$ is the complete global ML
tie set. The same conclusion holds after all streams are exhausted without requiring a
strict inequality.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
For each fixed $j$, the map $(e,b)\mapsto\operatorname{Ins}_j(y\oplus e,b)$ is a
bijection from the stream entries to $\{0,1\}^n$. A completely undiscovered $x$ has not
had its unique $j$th component emitted in any stream; therefore that component is at most
$\eta_j(t)$. Summing its $n$ components gives the first claim.

Every discovered codeword outside $\mathcal T_t^\star$ has score at most $s_t^\star$ by
definition. Every undiscovered codeword has score at most $U_{\mathrm{ind}}(t)$, which is
strictly below $s_t^\star$. Hence no undiscovered codeword can enter or tie the incumbent
set. If all streams are exhausted, no candidate remains undiscovered.
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}
The strict inequality is essential for certifying the complete tie set. A non-strict
comparison can certify only a selected maximizer under additional tie-handling logic.
The threshold principle itself has prior art; this theorem records the exact conditions
needed by the coding specialization.
\end{remark}


\section{Alignment-consistent shell bound}

Set $m=n-1$ and
\[
 g_j(d)=q_jp_s^d(1-p_s)^{m-d}.
\]
For a nonexhausted stream, let $r_j$ be the mismatch shell of its largest unprocessed
component; set $r_j=n$ after exhaustion and extend the notation by $g_j(n)=0$. Since
$p_s<1/2$, every $g_j$ is nonincreasing on $\{0,\ldots,m\}$.
Define
\begin{align*}
 \mathcal U_{\mathrm{sh}}(y,\mathbf r)
 &=\{x\in\{0,1\}^n:d_j(x,y)\ge r_j\text{ for all }j\},\\
 U_{\mathrm{AC}}(y,\mathbf r)
 &=\max_{x\in\mathcal U_{\mathrm{sh}}(y,\mathbf r)}
 \sum_{j=1}^{n}g_j(d_j(x,y)),
\end{align*}
where the maximum of the empty set is zero.

\begin{theorem}[Alignment-consistent shell certificate and optimized DP]
Every completely undiscovered candidate has likelihood at most
$U_{\mathrm{AC}}(y,\mathbf r)$, and
\[
 U_{\mathrm{AC}}(y,\mathbf r)
 \le\sum_{j=1}^{n}g_j(r_j)=U_{\mathrm{ind}}.
\]
The exact value of $U_{\mathrm{AC}}$ can be computed using $O(n^3)$ exact arithmetic operations and $O(n^2)$ rolling big-integer states.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
If a candidate had $d_j(x,y)<r_j$ in some stream, its unique component in that stream
would already have been emitted, so the candidate would have been discovered. Thus every
completely undiscovered candidate belongs to $\mathcal U_{\mathrm{sh}}$. If a stream is
exhausted, this feasible set is empty because $d_j\le m<n$, and the claim is immediate.
Otherwise monotonicity gives $g_j(d_j)\le g_j(r_j)$ term by term, proving both upper bounds.

It remains to compute the constrained maximum. Scan $x_1,\ldots,x_n$. At the first stage,
choose a value $a\in\{0,\ldots,m\}$ for $d_1$ and a bit $b=x_1$. Instead of retaining
$a$ forever, initialize the state
\[
 (b,d,R)=(b,a,a)
\]
with value $g_1(a)$, provided $a\ge r_1$. Here $R$ is the number of mismatches still
required among the suffix comparisons $(x_2,y_1),\ldots,(x_n,y_{n-1})$ in order for the
initial guess to equal the actual $d_1$.

At transition $j$, choose $c=x_{j+1}$ and set
\begin{align*}
 u&=\ind\{c\ne y_j\},\qquad v=\ind\{b\ne y_j\},\\
 R'&=R-u,\\
 d'&=d-u+v.
\end{align*}
Discard the transition if $R'<0$, $d'\notin\{0,\ldots,m\}$, or $d'<r_{j+1}$.
Otherwise update state $(c,d',R')$ by adding $g_{j+1}(d')$ and retain the largest value
among paths reaching that state. At the final stage accept only states with $R=0$.

For every word $x$, choosing $a=d_1(x,y)$ creates a unique feasible path and the recurrence
for $d_j$ gives exactly its objective value. Conversely, a terminal path with $R=0$ has
subtracted exactly $a$ suffix mismatches, so the initial guess equals the actual $d_1$;
therefore the path corresponds to a valid word in $\mathcal U_{\mathrm{sh}}$. The DP is
exact.

At any stage, $b$ has two values and both $d$ and $R$ have $O(n)$ values, so there are
$O(n^2)$ states. There are $n$ stages and two outgoing bit choices per state. Hence the algorithm uses $O(n^3)$ exact additions/comparisons and two rolling layers of $O(n^2)$ big-integer states.
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}[Strict improvement on a characterized family]
Assume $n\ge3$, uniform deletion, $0<p_s<1/2$, $y=0^{n-1}$, and $r_j=1$ for every alignment. Then
\begin{align*}
 U_{\mathrm{AC}}
 &=\frac{2}{n}p_s(1-p_s)^{n-2}
 +\frac{n-2}{n}p_s^2(1-p_s)^{n-3},\\
 U_{\mathrm{ind}}&=p_s(1-p_s)^{n-2},\\
 U_{\mathrm{ind}}-U_{\mathrm{AC}}
 &=\frac{n-2}{n}p_s(1-p_s)^{n-3}(1-2p_s)>0.
\end{align*}
\end{corollary}

\begin{proof}
For $y=0^{n-1}$, $d_j(x,y)=w_H(x)-x_j$. The constraints $d_j\ge1$ for all $j$ are
equivalent to $w_H(x)\ge2$. For a word of weight $w$, the aggregate score is
\[
 \frac1n\bigl[w p_s^{w-1}(1-p_s)^{n-w}
 +(n-w)p_s^w(1-p_s)^{n-1-w}\bigr].
\]
Writing $\rho=p_s/(1-p_s)<1$ shows that this expression decreases for $w\ge2$, so the
maximum is attained at $w=2$. Substitution gives $U_{\mathrm{AC}}$. The independent
bound is the sum of $n$ equal shell-one frontiers, and subtraction gives the strict gap.
\end{proof}

\section{Recurrence-relaxed certificate and schedule geometry}

Continue to write $m=n-1$ and
\[
 g_j(d)=q_jp_s^d(1-p_s)^{m-d},\qquad 0\le d\le m.
\]
For a frontier vector $\mathbf r=(r_1,\ldots,r_n)$, define
$\mathcal R(y,\mathbf r)$ as the set of pairs
$(x,\boldsymbol\delta)$ with $x\in\{0,1\}^n$ and
$\boldsymbol\delta\in\{0,\ldots,m\}^n$ such that
\begin{align}
 \delta_j&\ge r_j,\qquad 1\le j\le n,\label{eq:cr-threshold}\\
 \delta_{j+1}&=\delta_j-\ind\{x_{j+1}\ne y_j\}
                  +\ind\{x_j\ne y_j\},\qquad 1\le j<n.\label{eq:cr-recurrence}
\end{align}
Unlike a true mismatch vector, $\delta_1$ is not required to equal the suffix mismatch
count in \eqref{eq:dj}. Define
\[
 U_{\mathrm{CR}}(y,\mathbf r)=
 \max_{(x,\boldsymbol\delta)\in\mathcal R(y,\mathbf r)}
 \sum_{j=1}^n g_j(\delta_j),
\]
with the maximum of the empty set equal to zero.

\begin{theorem}[Recurrence-relaxed hierarchy and dynamic program]
For every observation and frontier vector,
\[
 U_{\mathrm{AC}}(y,\mathbf r)
 \le U_{\mathrm{CR}}(y,\mathbf r)
 \le U_{\mathrm{ind}}(\mathbf r):=\sum_{j=1}^n g_j(r_j),
\]
where a term with $r_j=n$ is interpreted as zero. The exact value of $U_{\mathrm{CR}}$ can be computed using $O(n^2)$ exact arithmetic operations and $O(n)$ rolling big-integer states.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Every true mismatch vector satisfies \eqref{eq:cr-recurrence}; dropping only its global
initial-value closure enlarges the feasible set, proving
$U_{\mathrm{AC}}\le U_{\mathrm{CR}}$. Since $p_s<1/2$, every $g_j$ is nonincreasing,
and \eqref{eq:cr-threshold} gives $g_j(\delta_j)\le g_j(r_j)$ term by term. This proves
the second inequality. If any $r_j=n$, both constrained feasible sets are empty because
$\delta_j\le m$.

For the dynamic program, at stage $j$ retain the largest accumulated objective for each
state $(x_j,\delta_j)$. Initialize every $x_1\in\{0,1\}$ and every
$\delta_1\in\{r_1,\ldots,m\}$. At a transition choose $x_{j+1}\in\{0,1\}$, compute
$\delta_{j+1}$ from \eqref{eq:cr-recurrence}, and discard the transition if it leaves
$\{0,\ldots,m\}$ or violates \eqref{eq:cr-threshold}. There are $O(n)$ possible mismatch
values, two current bits, $n$ stages, and two outgoing choices. Hence it uses $O(n^2)$ exact additions/comparisons and two rolling layers of $O(n)$ big-integer states.
\end{proof}

\begin{proposition}[Forward shell-completion degeneracy]
Fix $0\le s\le m-1$ and $0\le k\le n$. Suppose the frontier after an intermediate
forward-order shell completion is
\[
 r_j=\begin{cases}s+1,&j\le k,\\s,&j>k.\end{cases}
\]
Then $U_{\mathrm{CR}}(y,\mathbf r)=U_{\mathrm{ind}}(\mathbf r)$ for every observation,
every deletion distribution, and every $0<p_s<1/2$.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
The hierarchy already gives $U_{\mathrm{CR}}\le U_{\mathrm{ind}}$. It suffices to
construct a recurrence-relaxed path with $\delta_j=r_j$ for all $j$. If $k=0$ or $k=n$,
choose $x$ constant and initialize $\delta_1$ to the common frontier value; every
recurrence increment is zero. If $0<k<n$, choose
\[
 x_1=\cdots=x_k=y_k,\qquad
 x_{k+1}=\cdots=x_n=1-y_k,
\]
and initialize $\delta_1=s+1$. All recurrence increments vanish except at $j=k$, where
the increment is $-1$. Thus $\boldsymbol\delta=\mathbf r$, and its objective equals the
independent sum. The upper bound is therefore attained.
\end{proof}

\begin{proposition}[A strict nonmonotone-frontier example]
Let $n=3$, $y=(1,0)$, uniform deletion, $0<p_s<1/2$, and
$\mathbf r=(1,0,1)$. Then
\begin{align*}
 U_{\mathrm{AC}}(y,\mathbf r)=U_{\mathrm{CR}}(y,\mathbf r)&=p_s(1-p_s),\\
 U_{\mathrm{ind}}(\mathbf r)&=\frac{1-p_s^2}{3},\\
 U_{\mathrm{ind}}-U_{\mathrm{CR}}&=\frac{(1-p_s)(1-2p_s)}{3}>0.
\end{align*}
The frontier $\mathbf r=(1,0,1)$ occurs after completing shell zero in alignments
$1$ and $3$, before alignment $2$. Under uniform deletion these components have equal
probability, so the order $1,3,2$ remains a valid nonincreasing access order.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
If a relaxed path had $\delta_2=0$, then $\delta_1\ge1$ and a one-step change of at most
one force $\delta_1=1$ and a decrement of $-1$. Because $y_1=1$, this requires
$x_1=1$ and $x_2=0$. But $y_2=0$ and $x_2=0$ imply
$\delta_3-\delta_2=-\ind\{x_3\ne0\}\le0$, contradicting $\delta_3\ge1$.
Therefore every relaxed feasible path has $\delta_j\ge1$ for all $j$. Monotonicity of
$g_j$ gives an objective at most three shell-one components, namely $p_s(1-p_s)$ under
uniform deletion. The actual word $x=000$ has mismatch vector $(1,1,1)$, so both
$U_{\mathrm{AC}}$ and $U_{\mathrm{CR}}$ attain this value. The independent expression
and strict difference follow by direct subtraction.
\end{proof}


\section{What this document does and does not establish}

The mathematical statements above are independent of simulation. Exact arithmetic must
nonetheless compare the optimized DP with both exhaustive maximization and the original
four-coordinate DP, compare the recurrence relaxation with an independently implemented
exhaustive relaxation, and check both alignment schedules against exhaustive codeword-wise
ML on feasible complete worlds. Those checks detect implementation errors;
they do not create the proofs.

This document does not establish novelty of constrained rank aggregation, practical
latency benefit, final tail complexity, or suitability for a particular conference. The
Phase-2B computational gate must measure whether the strengthened bound reduces actual
search work enough to offset its evaluation cost.



\section{Phase-2B theorem-search status and claim boundary}

The strict inequality $U_{\mathrm{AC}}<U_{\mathrm{ind}}$ proves that deletion-alignment
compatibility contains information absent from independent-list thresholding.  It does
not by itself prove an asymptotic separation in emitted histories, membership probes, or
end-to-end time.  No infinite-family theorem of the form
$Q_{\mathrm{ind}}/Q_{\mathrm{AC}}=\Omega(n)$ is claimed in this version.  Phase~2B
therefore treats such a theorem as an optional strengthening and uses the compiled replay
as a kill gate for the practical-acceleration route.

The defensible mathematical claim is limited to the exact channel likelihood, the strict
best-history/aggregate-ML reversal family, the exact threshold certificate, the
alignment-consistent and recurrence-relaxed bound hierarchy, the exact dynamic programs,
and the characterized strict/degenerate frontier families.  Generic threshold
aggregation, expensive-predicate filtering, and the general path-versus-string distinction
remain credited prior art.
