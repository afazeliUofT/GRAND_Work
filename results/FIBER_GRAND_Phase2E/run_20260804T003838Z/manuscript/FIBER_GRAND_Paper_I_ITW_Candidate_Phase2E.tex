\title{FIBER-GRAND: Exact Shell Certification for Aggregate-ML Decoding\
with One Deletion and Substitutions}
\author{\IEEEauthorblockN{Ali Fazeli}
\IEEEauthorblockA{Department of Electrical and Computer Engineering\\
University of Toronto, Toronto, Canada}}

\begin{abstract}
Exact likelihoods for insertion/deletion/substitution channels are obtained by summing over
compatible synchronization paths, so the first codeword reached by a single likely history need
not be maximum likelihood (ML). We isolate the finite-block model of exactly one uniformly located
deletion followed by independent binary symmetric substitutions and an arbitrary binary code
accessed through membership tests. We give an explicit two-codeword family in which the largest
deletion-alignment component and the aggregate-ML codeword strictly reverse, with threshold
$np_s>1+2p_s^2$. We then specialize inverse-noise search to aggregate candidate likelihoods. Its
channel-specific ingredients are a closed-form upper bound on every word unseen after a completed
substitution shell, an exact complete-tie stopping certificate, and a code-independent
realization-dependent stopping bound. Standard Hamming-volume asymptotics then give the usual
$h_2(p_s)$ high-probability query exponent as a corollary, not as a new entropy law. A random-code
occupancy calculation predicts the observed number of completely scored codewords. Across 996 exact
finite-length trials, all exact comparators agreed; at $n=32$ the median number of completely scored
codewords was $1.5$--$13.5$. Operation counts and wall-clock time are reported separately, and no
universal latency or code-specific optimality claim is made.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
For variable-length channels, the likelihood of an output is obtained by marginalizing over
compatible edit paths; this principle and efficient synchronization-channel likelihood computation
are classical \cite{bahl1975decoding,davey2001reliable}. In the pure-deletion limit, ML decoding is
governed by alignment or embedding multiplicity \cite{sabary2020error}. This differs from GRAND on
an additive channel, where likelihood-ordered noise guessing makes the first codebook hit ML
\cite{duffy2019grand}. Thus, GRAND's first-hit argument does not transfer unchanged when multiple
synchronization histories coalesce into one candidate word.

Related work includes ML formulations and certified optimization for binary codes with deletion
synchronization errors \cite{yang2020decoding}, sequential synchronization decoding
\cite{banerjee2022sequential}, and a shift-compensating minimum distance over one-deletion
alignments that is linearly computable \cite{gershon2022genomic}. Most-probable-path versus
most-probable-string distinctions and incumbent-versus-unfinished-mass bounds are also established
in probabilistic automata \cite{higuera2013computing}. Generic threshold stopping is classical
\cite{fagin2003optimal}; recent guessing-codeword work likewise proves ML under appropriate
incumbent bounds \cite{wang2025guessing}. We therefore do not claim a new aggregate-likelihood
principle, a new deletion distance, or a new generic stopping paradigm.

The narrow contributions are as follows. First, we give a strict binary one-deletion-plus-BSC
construction with an exact reversal threshold. Second, for this channel we derive a closed-form
unseen-word bound and a complete-tie exact certificate for membership-based inverse search. Third,
we prove a realization-dependent stopping theorem and finite-confidence work caps. The associated
$h_2(p_s)$ exponent is explicitly presented as standard Hamming-volume asymptotics applied after
that stopping result. Fourth, we provide exact finite-length evidence and a random-code occupancy
benchmark that explains the observed scoring work. The model is fixed to one uniform deletion and
hard BSC substitutions; multiple edits, soft information, code design, and hardware are outside
scope.

\section{Channel law and a strict alignment-component reversal}
Let $X=(X_1,\ldots,X_n)\in\{0,1\}^n$. Independent substitutions with probability
$0\le p_s<1/2$ act before exactly one uniformly located deletion. The observation
$Y\in\{0,1\}^{m}$ has $m=n-1$. The nonempty code $\C\subseteq\{0,1\}^n$ has a uniform prior and is
accessed through $\one\{x\in\C\}$. For candidate $x$ and observation $y$, define
\begin{equation}
 d_j(x,y)=\sum_{i<j}\one\{x_i\ne y_i\}+\sum_{i>j}\one\{x_i\ne y_{i-1}\}.
 \label{eq:dj}
\end{equation}
Conditioning on the deletion coordinate gives the established aggregate form specialized to this
channel,
\begin{equation}
 W(y\mid x)=\frac1n\sum_{j=1}^{n}p_s^{d_j(x,y)}(1-p_s)^{m-d_j(x,y)}. \label{eq:likelihood}
\end{equation}
All $d_j$ are obtained in $O(n)$ arithmetic operations from
\begin{equation}
 d_{j+1}=d_j-\one\{x_{j+1}\ne y_j\}+\one\{x_j\ne y_j\}. \label{eq:slide}
\end{equation}
The quantity below is the largest \emph{deletion-alignment component} after the unobserved
substitution on the deleted coordinate is marginalized. A fully specified pre-deletion history
adds the same maximizing factor $1-p_s$ to every candidate and does not change the ordering.

\begin{theorem}[Strict component/aggregate-ML reversal]\label{thm:reversal}
For $n\ge4$ and $0<p_s<1/2$, let
\[
 y=0^{n-3}10,\quad x_A=0^{n-3}101,\quad x_B=0^n,\quad \C=\{x_A,x_B\}.
\]
The largest deletion-alignment component strictly favors $x_A$, while aggregate ML favors $x_B$
if and only if
\begin{equation}
 np_s>1+2p_s^2. \label{eq:condition}
\end{equation}
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}[Proof sketch]
Deleting the final symbol of $x_A$ requires no survivor substitution, while every alignment of
$x_B$ requires one. The mismatch multiplicities of $x_A$ at distances $0,1,2,3$ are
$1,1,1,n-3$. Substitution in \eqref{eq:likelihood} gives
\[
 \frac{n\{W(y\mid x_B)-W(y\mid x_A)\}}{(1-p_s)^{n-4}}
 =(1-2p_s)(np_s-1-2p_s^2),
\]
which proves the statement. Complete proofs are in the supplement.
\end{proof}
At the experimental point $p_s=0.05$, \eqref{eq:condition} holds exactly for integer $n\ge21$;
therefore this family reverses at $n=24,32$ but not at $n=16$. The general path-versus-aggregate
phenomenon is known; the claim here is only the explicit strict binary family and threshold.

\section{Shell-certified exact aggregate search}
For $z\in\{0,1\}^{m}$, let $\I(z)$ be its binary one-insertion sphere. Its classical cardinality is
$n+1$ \cite{levenshtein1966binary}. Define
\begin{equation}
 h_y(x)=\min_j d_j(x,y). \label{eq:hy}
\end{equation}
Up to orientation, this is the known minimum one-deletion alignment distance used in
\cite{gershon2022genomic}. The immediate inverse-enumeration property needed here is: if shell $s$
enumerates every error pattern $e$ of weight $s$, forms $z=y\oplus e$, and generates $\I(z)$,
then candidate $x$ first appears at shell $h_y(x)$.

\begin{algorithm}[t]
\caption{Shell-certified one-deletion FIBER-GRAND}\label{alg:fiber}
\begin{algorithmic}[1]
\State $\mathsf{seen}\gets\emptyset$; no incumbent
\For{$s=0,1,\ldots,m$}
  \ForAll{$e\in\{0,1\}^m$ with $\wt(e)=s$}
    \State $z\gets y\oplus e$
    \ForAll{$x\in\I(z)$ using the $n+1$ distinct generator}
      \If{$x\notin\mathsf{seen}$}
        \State insert $x$ into $\mathsf{seen}$ and query $x\in\C$
        \If{$x\in\C$}
          \State evaluate the complete score \eqref{eq:likelihood} using \eqref{eq:slide}
          \State update the incumbent score and complete tie set
        \EndIf
      \EndIf
    \EndFor
  \EndFor
  \If{an incumbent exists and $[s=m$ or incumbent $>U_s]$}
    \State \Return the incumbent and complete ML tie set
  \EndIf
\EndFor
\end{algorithmic}
\end{algorithm}

For $0<p_s<1/2$, after completing shell $s<m$, every unseen candidate has
$d_j\ge s+1$ for all $j$. Hence its \emph{aggregate} likelihood obeys the code-independent bound
\begin{equation}
 W(y\mid x_{\rm unseen})\le U_s:=p_s^{s+1}(1-p_s)^{m-s-1}. \label{eq:unseen}
\end{equation}
The factor $1/n$ in \eqref{eq:likelihood} cancels the sum over $n$ alignments, so no extra factor
$n$ appears in $U_s$.

\begin{theorem}[Exact unseen-shell certificate]\label{thm:certificate}
After shell $s<m$, every unseen word has likelihood at most $U_s$. If a completely scored incumbent
has likelihood strictly greater than $U_s$, the incumbent set is the complete global ML tie set.
Algorithm~\ref{alg:fiber} always terminates and returns exact aggregate ML.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
First appearance at $h_y(x)$ implies $d_j\ge s+1$ for every alignment of an unseen word. Since
$p_s/(1-p_s)<1$, every term in \eqref{eq:likelihood} is at most $U_s$, and so is their average.
Strict comparison excludes both a better unseen codeword and an unseen tie. At $s=m$, every binary
length-$n$ word has been generated.
\end{proof}
Strictness is necessary for a complete tie set. For example, at $n=4$, $y=001$, $p_s=1/3$, and
$\C=\{0010,0000\}$, the first word is seen at shell zero, the second is unseen, and both have
likelihood $U_0=4/27$. A non-strict certificate would omit an ML tie. At $p_s=0$, shell zero
contains every positive-likelihood word; at $p_s=1/2$, strict shell decay disappears and complete
tie certification requires exhaustion.

\section{Stopping, finite work, and the standard entropy corollary}
Let $T$ be the realized number of substitutions among the $m$ surviving symbols, let
$\rho=p_s/(1-p_s)$, and define
\begin{equation}
 L_n(p_s)=\min\{\ell\ge1:\rho^\ell<1/n\}
 =\left\lfloor\log_{(1-p_s)/p_s}n\right\rfloor+1. \label{eq:Ln}
\end{equation}

\begin{theorem}[Realization-dependent stopping]\label{thm:stop}
For $0<p_s<1/2$, Algorithm~\ref{alg:fiber} stops no later than
\begin{equation}
 S\le \min\{m,T+L_n(p_s)-1\}. \label{eq:stop}
\end{equation}
Consequently,
\begin{equation}
 Q_{\rm gen},Q_{\rm mem}\le(n+1)\Vol(m,S),\qquad Q_{\rm score}\le Q_{\rm mem}, \label{eq:work}
\end{equation}
where $\Vol(m,s)=\sum_{w=0}^{\min\{m,s\}}\binom mw$.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}[Proof sketch]
The transmitted word is discovered by shell $T$ and contributes at least
$n^{-1}p_s^T(1-p_s)^{m-T}$. Put $s_0=T+L_n-1$. If $s_0\ge m$, finite exhaustion gives
$S\le m$. Otherwise $U_{s_0}$ is defined and its ratio to that component is
$n\rho^{L_n}<1$, so the strict certificate fires by $s_0$. Counting $n+1$ distinct insertion
outputs per error vector gives \eqref{eq:work}.
\end{proof}
If $\tau_\delta$ is the smallest binomial quantile satisfying
$\Pr[T\le\tau_\delta]\ge1-\delta$, then with probability at least $1-\delta$,
\begin{equation}
 Q_{\rm mem}\le(n+1)\Vol\!\left(m,\min\{m,\tau_\delta+L_n-1\}\right). \label{eq:finitecap}
\end{equation}

For fixed $p_s$ and any $c>0$, Hoeffding concentration and standard Hamming-ball asymptotics give,
with probability at least $1-n^{-c}$,
\begin{equation}
 Q_{\rm mem},Q_{\rm score}\le2^{n(h_2(p_s)+o(1))}. \label{eq:exponent}
\end{equation}
This exponent mechanism is the classical BSC typical-volume/GRAND mechanism
\cite{duffy2019grand}, transported to the exact synchronization decoder after Theorem~\ref{thm:stop};
it is not claimed as a new entropy law. Formally, with
$a_n=p_s+\sqrt{c\ln n/(2m)}+(L_n-1)/m$, the high-probability event has $S/m\le a_n$,
$a_n=p_s+o(1)$, and for all sufficiently large $n$, $a_n<1/2$. Monotonicity and continuity give
$\Vol(m,S)\le2^{m h_2(a_n)}=2^{n(h_2(p_s)+o(1))}$.

\section{Random-code occupancy benchmark and exact evidence}
The large reduction in complete scoring can be predicted without attributing it to code structure.
\begin{proposition}[Uniform-code occupancy benchmark]\label{prop:occupancy}
Fix a queried set $A\subseteq\{0,1\}^n$ of size $G$ containing the transmitted word $x_0$.
Let $\C$ be a uniformly chosen $M$-word codebook conditioned on $x_0\in\C$. Then
\begin{equation}
 \mathbb E[|A\cap\C|\mid A,x_0]
 =1+(G-1)\frac{M-1}{2^n-1}. \label{eq:occupancy}
\end{equation}
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
Each of the other $G-1$ queried words belongs to the remaining uniformly selected $M-1$ codewords
with probability $(M-1)/(2^n-1)$; linearity of expectation proves the result.
\end{proof}
This is a benchmark for a fixed query set, not a theorem for structured codes or for data conditioned
on a stopping event. At $n=32$ and shell one, the generation cap is
$G_1=(n+1)\Vol(31,1)=1056$. Equation~\eqref{eq:occupancy} predicts mean complete-score counts
$1.52$, $5.12$, and $17.48$ for $k=21,24,26$, respectively, close to the observed medians.

The frozen campaign comprised 996 exact trials: 896 natural and 100 forced-weight stress trials,
with zero validation violation or censoring. Deduplicated shell search and the history-pair search
returned identical ML scores and complete tie sets; 384 exhaustive and 432 completed generic
branch-and-bound comparisons also agreed. At $n=32$, all five cells had median stop shell one.
Only 16 exhaustive $n=32$ timing calibrations were run, all at $k=21$; their pooled median
exhaustive/FIBER time ratio was 465.6. Branch-and-bound was faster in one of five $n=32$ cells and
slower in four, so no universal exact-decoder speed claim follows.

The classical $n+1$ generator reduced attempted insertions by about $1.94\times$ relative to $2n$
history pairs, but failed its preregistered wall-clock gate. Selected first-shell representatives
differed from selected aggregate-ML representatives in many trials, but the tie sets always
overlapped; zero sampled case had disjoint tie sets. Theorem~\ref{thm:reversal}, not empirical
prevalence, supplies the strict pathwise failure. High ML block-error rates in these high-rate cells
are channel/code behavior, not decoder incorrectness, because all exact comparators agree.

\section{Conclusion}
The exact aggregate-likelihood principle, minimum deletion-alignment metric, membership-oracle
interface, and generic incumbent stopping logic are prior art. The defensible one-deletion results
are narrower: an explicit strict component/aggregate reversal, the closed-form unseen-shell bound
$U_s$, complete-tie exact certification using that bound, and the realization-dependent stop
$S\le T+L_n-1$. Standard entropy-volume arguments then yield the usual high-probability exponent.
The occupancy benchmark quantitatively explains the finite scoring savings. Together these form a
correct, auditable workshop-scale contribution for arbitrary membership-testable binary codes under
the stated channel, without implying a new universal synchronization-decoding paradigm.
